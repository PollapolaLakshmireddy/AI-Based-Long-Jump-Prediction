🏃‍♂️ The 5 Biomechanical Phases of the Long Jump

Every successful jump depends on energy preservation and optimal kinematic conversion across 5 sequential phases.

01

1. Approach Runway

Accelerating down the 35–45m runway (16–22 strides). The goal is reaching maximum controllable sprint velocity with tall, upright posture while maintaining visual focus past the board.

Optimal Speed 9.5 – 11.0 m/s
02

2. Penultimate Stride

The second-to-last stride lowers the athlete's Center of Mass (CoM) by 3–6 cm through a flat, rolling foot contact without deceleration, priming elastic tendon loading for takeoff.

CoM Drop -3 to -6 cm
03

3. Takeoff Board Lift

Instantaneous foot strike (0.11–0.13s) converting ~85% horizontal speed into vertical trajectory. Active lead knee drive to 90° and full takeoff leg extension (160°–175°).

Optimal Launch Angle 18° – 23°
04

4. In-Flight Kinematics

Utilizing either the Hitch-Kick (pedaling action) or Hang style to counteract natural forward rotational momentum imparted at the board and prepare the body for maximum landing reach.

Flight Airtime 0.65 – 0.85 sec
05

5. Sand Pit Landing

Jackknife body fold with legs extended forward. Heel strike initiates active knee flexion, sweeping the hips forward and sideways past the impression mark to prevent falling back.

Landing Stability > 90% Target
